## CHAPTER I: GENERAL PROVISION

### Article 1. Governing Scope and Regulated Entities
This document specifies rules and regulations that govern educational arrangements, examinations, assessment, grading, and graduation requirements based on an academic credit system for full-time undergraduate students at VinUniversity. These rules and regulations also apply to exchange and visiting students.

### Article 2. Medium of Instruction and Assessment
1. Unless otherwise approved by the University for a specific course or program of study, English is the medium of instruction (including lectures, tutorials, and laboratories) at the University. Individual instruction or consultation may be in any language.
2. The College of Arts and Sciences may teach in Vietnamese and use Vietnamese reading materials for the required courses of the Ministry of Education and Training (MOET). In such a case, the materials will be provided in English to students as and when requested.
3. The College of Health Sciences:
   - Course and Clinical Rotation Language Use: Option to conduct courses and clinical rotations in Vietnamese when deemed appropriate. Formal materials in English, with bilingual format where needed.
   - International students must demonstrate Vietnamese language proficiency at level B2 (CEFR/Vietnamese 6-level framework) before entering the Medical Doctor Program in Year 4.
   - National practice licensing exams for medical and nursing graduates are conducted in Vietnamese.

### Article 3. Academic Program and Syllabi
1. An academic program involves degree levels, educational objectives, learning outcomes, required credits, academic plan, teaching methodology, and assessment mode.
2. Structure includes General Education requirements, College/Faculty requirements, major requirements, free electives, and/or a minor.
3. Syllabi define credit hours, prerequisites, assessment methods, coursebooks, and reference materials.

### Article 4. Course and Credit
1. A credit determines academic workload. 1 credit is equivalent to 50 hours of total study:
   - 1 credit = 15 contact hours for lecture or classroom instruction.
   - 30 - 45 contact hours for practicum and laboratory courses.
   - 45 - 90 contact hours for fieldwork (industry, community, hospital).
   - 45 - 60 hours for thesis, capstone project, or internship.
   - Students spend at least 30 self-study hours per credit during the semester.
2. Non-credit bearing courses: e.g. National Defense Education, Leadership Bootcamp, Physical Education/Healthy Lifestyle.

### Article 5. Class Meeting Times
1. Classes start from 8:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. Monday to Friday. Evening/weekend sessions may run up to 9:25 p.m. upon Dean/Registrar approval.
2. Standard class duration: 50 or 75 minutes.

### Article 6. Student Academic Performance Evaluation
1. Evaluated after each semester based on earned credits, Semester GPA (SGPA), Cumulative academic load, and Cumulative GPA (CGPA) on a 4.0 scale.

## CHAPTER II: EDUCATIONAL ARRANGEMENTS

### Article 7. Academic Planning and Duration of Study
1. Planned curriculum duration:
   - Not less than 120 credits for a 4-year undergraduate program.
   - Not less than 180 credits for a 6-year program (Medical Doctor).
2. Academic year: Two main semesters (Fall and Spring), each with 15 weeks of actual study, 1 week of Independent Study Week, and 2 weeks of final exams.
3. Maximum study duration: Planned education duration plus 4 main semesters for 4-year program, or plus 6 main semesters for 5-6 year program.

### Article 11. Study Load
1. Normal full-time load: 30 credits per academic year. Full-time status requires at least 80% of normal load in a semester (minimum 12 credits).
2. Normal semester load: At least 14 credits (for students in good standing). Between 10 and 14 credits for students with poor academic standing.
3. Overload: 18 - 22 credits subject to Academic Advisor / Program Director review. More than 22 credits requires College Dean approval.
4. Summer session: up to 9 credits.

### Article 12. Course Add, Drop, and Withdrawal
1. Course Add: No later than the close of the 10th business day of regular semester (5th business day of mini semesters).
2. Course Drop: No later than the close of the 15th business day of regular semester (10th business day of Summer semester). Dropped courses do not appear on transcript.
3. Course Withdrawal: Permitted after drop deadline up to 30% of study time for the course. Results in a "W" grade (no GPA penalty). Maximum withdrawal limit: 18 credits over the entire program of study.

### Article 14. Re-Study / Improvement Study
1. Students who fail a required course must retake it or an approved equivalent course.
2. Repeating a passed course to improve GPA: Only one repeat attempt is permitted. The most recent grade is calculated into GPA; previous grade is excluded from GPA.

### Article 16. Student Classification and Academic Standing
1. Class standing (Business, Engineering & Computer Science, Arts & Sciences):
   - Freshman: Below 30 credits.
   - Sophomore: 30 - 59 credits.
   - Junior: 60 - 89 credits.
   - Senior: 90 or more credits.
2. Academic standing based on CGPA:
   - Excellent: 3.60 - 4.00
   - Very good: 3.20 - 3.59
   - Good: 2.50 - 3.19
   - Marginal: 2.00 - 2.49
   - Unsatisfactory (At risk): Below 2.00

### Article 17. Academic Warning, Probation and Dismissal
1. Academic Warning criteria: Cumulative GPA under 1.20 (freshman), 1.40 (sophomore), 1.60 (junior), 1.80 (senior); OR semester GPA under 0.80 (first semester) or under 1.00 (subsequent semesters); OR total failed credits in semester > 50% or cumulative failed credits > 24 credits.
2. Academic Probation: Warning in two consecutive semesters.
3. Academic Dismissal: Exceeding maximum allowed study time, OR receiving academic warning 3 times in total.

### Article 18. Academic Honors (Dean's List)
- Minimum SGPA 3.60 (Very Good), minimum 12 letter-graded credits, no failed courses, no major disciplinary misconduct.

### Article 19. Double Degree
- Eligibility: CGPA $\ge 2.50$ (Good or higher), completed at least Year 1, registered at least 2 years prior to tentative graduation.

## CHAPTER IV: GRADING POLICY

### Article 25. Grading System
- A: 4.0, A-: 3.7
- B+: 3.3, B: 3.0, B-: 2.7
- C+: 2.3, C: 2.0, C-: 1.7
- D+: 1.3, D: 1.0, D-: 0.7
- F: 0.0 (Fail)
- Passing grades: A through D-. Operational grades: CR, NCR, I (Incomplete), IP (In-Progress), W (Withdrawn), T (Transfer), Z (Exemption).

## CHAPTER V: GRADUATION & AWARDS

### Article 28. Recognition of Graduation
- Conditions: Minimum credits completed, General Education / VinCore requirements met, English proficiency satisfied, Major requirements completed, no outstanding "I" grade, CGPA $\ge 2.00/4.00$, no criminal convictions.
